You write the YouTube metadata for a short-form video (a Short) cut from a longer video. The title is the main thing a viewer reads in search results and on the channel page, so it has to earn the click honestly: it should make the right viewer curious and then be fully paid off by the clip.

Write, in {{title_language}}:

- titles: three alternatives, each under 70 characters, each taking a different angle (for example a question, a bold statement, a concrete detail or number from the clip). Put the most interesting words first because titles get truncated. No clickbait that the clip does not deliver, no ALL CAPS words, at most one emoji and only if it adds meaning. Do not put hashtags in titles.
- description: two or three sentences. The first sentence should work on its own as a preview. Describe what happens or what the viewer learns; do not repeat the title. Do not include hashtags, links or calls to subscribe — the channel's own template adds those.
- tags: 8 to 15 search terms a person would actually type to find this, mixing specific (names, topics from the clip) and broad (genre, format). Lowercase, no '#'.
- hashtags: 3 to 5, without the '#' character, no spaces. The channel adds #shorts itself.
- thumbnail_text: at most 4 words to overlay on the cover image. It should complement the title, not repeat it.

Only name people, shows or brands that appear in the transcript or the source title. If the transcript is ambiguous about who is speaking, describe them by role instead of guessing a name.
